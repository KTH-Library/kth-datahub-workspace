# Access to digital infrastructures for research


There are numerous research infrastructures available for academic research and
some of them fall under the category of *digital infrastructures* for research.

## Interpretation of *digital infrastructures* in a research context

Infrastructure is commonly defined as the basic physical and organizational structures,
systems, and services needed for a country, city, or organization to function properly.
Usually, infrastructure is expensive to build and maintain and thus its cost
needs to be distributed between many users.

When referring to *digital infrastructure* the focus is sometimes narrowed down
to only the technical infrastructure underlying digital systems and services.
But the infrastructures needed for research are also organizational structures
providing access to research services and are highly dependent on people with
specialized digital skills as well.
We therefore employ the human-centric definition of digital infrastructures in a
research context provided by the [Digital Creativity and Cultures Hub (DCCH)](https://dcch.leeds.ac.uk)
at the University of Leeds:

> Digital Research Infrastructure (DRI) encompasses the computational tools, services and
> support systems that enable researchers to conduct their work. This includes
> high-performance computing resources, research software and platforms, data storage
> and management systems, as well as the training, documentation, and community support
> that help researchers use these tools effectively. It's the entire ecosystem that
> makes computational research possible – not just the technology itself, but
> the **knowledge** and **networks** that make it **accessible and useful**.


## How do I access services on academic infrastructures?

In general, you often have to *apply* to access resources and services from research infrastructures.
If you are eligible you usually don't have to allocate money from your research budget
to use existing services.

The national digital research infrastructure has some known pain points:

+ In many cases you cannot login with your KTH-ID and need to register a local account at each infrastructure.
+ When using public cloud services, you must have a plan for for where your research data will end up
  when you wrap your research. Also, there is unfortunately no compliance service to provide you
  advice on what use-cases are suitable for public cloud offerings.
+ There is currently no standardized recommended way to transfer data to or from NAISS compute services.
+ There is currently no national platform for version control of code.
