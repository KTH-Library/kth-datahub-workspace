    Access to digital infrastructures for research

    There are numerous research infrastructures available for academic research and some of them fall under the category of digital infrastructures for research.

    Interpretation of digital infrastructures in a research context

    Infrastructure is commonly defined as the basic physical and organizational structures, systems, and services needed for a country, city, or organization to function properly. Usually, infrastructure is expensive to build and maintain and thus its cost needs to be distributed between many users.

    When referring to digital infrastructure the focus is sometimes narrowed down to only the technical infrastructure underlying digital systems and services. But the infrastructures needed for research are also organizational structures providing access to research services and are highly dependent on people with specialized digital skills as well. We therefore employ the human-centric definition of digital infrastructures in a research context provided by the Digital Creativity and Cultures Hub (DCCH) at the University of Leeds:

    Digital Research Infrastructure (DRI) encompasses the computational tools, services and support systems that enable researchers to conduct their work. This includes high-performance computing resources, research software and platforms, data storage and management systems, as well as the training, documentation, and community support that help researchers use these tools effectively. It's the entire ecosystem that makes computational research possible – not just the technology itself, but the knowledge and networks that make it accessible and useful.

    How do I access services on academic infrastructures?

    In general, you often have to apply to access resources and services from research infrastructures. If you are eligible you usually don't have to allocate money from your research budget to use existing services.

    List of current access points to digital research infrastructures in Sweden:

    Infrastructure Description Service offering or application for access
    InfraVis Access to highly skilled staff for support in data analysis and visualisation. KTH has a contact point at Visualization centre. Apply for service and support at national level at the InfraVis support form
    SND Access to specialists, and digital repositories as FAIR data access points where you can deposit, search and find research data. Provide support on discovery, findability and reuse of such data. https://snd.se/en/services
    SUNET Access to global network infrastructure (Internet) and national access point to global academic infrastructures via access services provided via the SWAMID federation that in some cases enable you to connect to academic services world-wide with your KTH-SSO service. https://www.sunet.se/services
    storage? No national infrastructure with a clear responsibility for access to storage services exists and this makes the storage landscape very fragmented. In practice it is an enormous challenge to select appropriate useful storage. Currently, there is no national access point to approved storage services. So access points to storage is distributed across your own device and external disk drives, research group- or department-servers, IT department disk or server storage, PDC hot storage and library service with medium-term storage. In some cases more domain-specific infrastructures can provide you with storage services as well. The IT department can currently provide access point to commercial project storage services provided by Microsoft-owned infrastructure or KTH-internal storage infrastructure, but access to the only available project storage service provided by SUNET is limited to 25 MB. Understaffing both when it comes to staff for support, management and compliance services make this a key area for improvement.
    NAISS Access to allocated compute time at HPC clusters and application specialists that know how to customize software and jobs for that runtime environment. Support service available at national level where you also apply for allocated time. https://www.naiss.se/resources
    Other Swedish research infrastructures Many traditional research infrastructures focus more on providing access to specific equipment and instruments, but since large-scale instrumentation and test-beds increasingly get connected to both digital infrastructure and services, many of those data-producing infrastructures provide an increasing amount of data and digital services Find relevant services via those infrastructures via this list available research infrastructures in Sweden with funding from Swedish Science council and research infrastructures and testbeds with other funding here [does such a compiled list exist?], and KTH research infrastructures
    EU research infrastructures Some consortia of infrastructures have domain-specific services you can access, but it requires some browsing around to find useful services. There is also a concept EU node of a European digital cloud service for research. https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/research-infrastructure.html

    The national digital research infrastructure has some known pain points:

    • In many cases you cannot login with your KTH-ID and need to register a local account at each infrastructure.
    • When using public cloud services, you must have a plan for for where your research data will end up when you wrap your research. Also, there is unfortunately no compliance service to provide you advice on what use-cases are suitable for public cloud offerings.
    • There is currently no standardized recommended way to transfer data to or from NAISS compute services.
    • There is currently no national platform for version control of code.
